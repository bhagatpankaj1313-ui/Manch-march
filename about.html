About Manch March

Manch March is a 2D top-down stealth-action game. You lead a peaceful march toward the fictional Kranti Maidan while police lines try to shut it down. The twist that drives every level: your allies are your power. Each ally in the chain adds strength, and losing the whole chain means the march is busted.

What kind of game is it?

It is a strategy-action mix. You move, aim and fight in real time, but winning is about positioning, timing and protecting the chain — not just shooting. Barricades, walls, rallies and special powers let you shape each encounter, and the campaign grows harder level by level, with bigger maps, heavier police pressure and the VAJRA boss vehicle on key stages.

Who made it?

Manch March is an original game by Pankaj Bhagat, a computer science student and independent game developer from Ludhiana, Punjab, India. The game is developed as a single self-contained web game, so it runs entirely in the browser with no download and no account needed.

Is it based on anything real?

No. The story, characters, places (including Kranti Maidan), organisations and artwork are fictional and created for this game. The game uses a light, non-lethal tone: opponents are knocked out or dispersed, never killed. Any resemblance to real places, events or people is coincidence.

Where can I play it?

Right here, free, on the Play page — on a phone, tablet or computer. An Android version also exists for play on the go. New levels and polish land regularly; the version on this site is the current web release.
